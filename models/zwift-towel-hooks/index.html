Zwift Ride container — towel hooks

A printed add-on for the Zwift Ride bottle container: a hook on each side wall, merged into the container mesh so the whole thing prints as one piece. The hooks add 28 mm of width and print without supports.

towel-hook-tuned.stl2.7 MB

As shipped, and tuned

Left is the container as Zwift ships it. Right is the same part with a hook merged into each side wall, Ø20 vents cut through the side panels, and the hook's root widened into a 4 mm collar. With the cameras linked, dragging either tile keeps both on the same angle — the only difference you are looking at is the part.

The hook itself

The drawn hook has a 3 mm under-lip radius and a straight root. The printed one has a 6 mm radius and a 4 mm collar that spreads the peg's load 40 mm along the wall. The section is the whole difference.

Why it is built this way

A towel on a hook is a peel load: the pull is perpendicular to the surface the hook sits on. On a 3 mm panel that is the failure mode — the hook survives and the wall cracks out around it. So the hooks sit at the top of the side walls, where the pull goes into the rim instead of bending a free panel, and the root is a wide pad rather than a thin stem.

Layer lines run horizontally, which puts the arm's bending tension along the layers instead of across them. The web under the arm is 45°, the stop at its end is 45°, and the only horizontal face below the arm is an 8 mm strip that bridges — so the part prints without supports.

The source mesh is not manifold: 3 934 self-intersecting triangles and an uncapped face on the central boss. Bambu Studio repairs that silently, a boolean engine will not, so the hook was merged into a mesh repaired by filling the open boundary and nothing else. A heavier repair bought watertightness by deleting the embossing and a Z cut-out, which is why the mesh is proven against frozen probes rather than trusted after one render.
