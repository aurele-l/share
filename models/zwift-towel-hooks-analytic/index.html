Zwift Ride container — proper analytic rebuild

The same printed part as the towel-hook container — both bins, the clamp, sixteen Ø20 vents and the two hook pegs — rebuilt from measured planes, cylinders and tangent fillets instead of lofted mesh sections. Every wall is one face, every fillet one cylinder, every vent one bore. Drag the tiles: with the cameras linked, the only difference you are looking at is the construction.

Why it is built this way

The first analytic attempt lofted measured mesh sections and baked every mesh wiggle into 7,116 spline patches, which tessellate as visible triangulation. This rebuild measures the walls as planes, the fillets as cylinders and the rim as bars, then extrudes and fuses them — the faces you see outlined here are the actual CAD faces.

Honestly reported: the rebuild ties the lofted version on mean deviation (0.15 vs 0.17 mm) and trails it at p95 (1.02 vs 1.00 mm, identical sampling). The remaining hotspots are the divider jaw transition, the tip nose rounding and the unclaimed embossing — named in the decisions log, not hidden.

The printable file is the STEP: slice that, not the reference STL. The rail-fit gauge print and a towel on the hook are still unverified — theory until then.
